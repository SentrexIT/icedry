The Science

HOW DRY ICE BLASTING WORKS

CO₂ dry ice blasting uses solid carbon dioxide pellets fired through compressed air to remove contaminants. The pellets freeze the surface layer on contact, causing it to fracture and lift — then the CO₂ converts directly to gas and disappears.

01
❄️

CO₂ PELLETS

Solid CO₂ pellets at approximately −78°C are fed into the blasting unit and propelled through compressed air at high velocity.

02
💨

KINETIC IMPACT

On impact, kinetic energy from the pellets dislodges and loosens surface contaminants such as grease, paint, soot, and biological matter.

03
🌡️

THERMAL SHOCK

The extreme cold causes contaminants to contract and fracture away from the substrate at a different rate, breaking the adhesion bond.

04
☁️

SUBLIMATION

CO₂ converts directly from solid to gas — no blast media remains. Only the original contaminant needs to be swept or collected.

Manufacturing

Dry Ice Cleaning for Manufacturing Equipment

Common problem: Production moulds, conveyors, and tooling accumulate grease, release agents, and residue. Traditional cleaning requires disassembly, causing planned downtime of hours or days — plus chemical handling and blast media disposal.

Dry ice blasting can clean many production assets in place, often during planned maintenance windows without full strip-down. Only the original contamination needs disposal — no added blast media means less secondary waste handling.

  • Often reduces time to restart vs. strip-clean-reassemble cycle
  • No added blast media to collect and dispose of
  • Suitable for use around sensitive production sensors and components (subject to assessment)
  • Out-of-hours cleaning available to fit planned maintenance schedules

Electrical & Utilities

Dry Ice Blasting for Electrical and Control Panels

Common problem: Switchgear, control panels, and transformers collect conductive contamination and carbonised deposits. Water-based cleaning is not viable, and chemical cleaning risks residue on sensitive components.

Dry ice blasting can be used on electrical equipment when properly isolated and assessed for suitability. CO₂ is non-conductive and leaves no residue — removing contamination without introducing moisture.

  • No moisture introduced — eliminates drying time and associated risk
  • Can clean in-situ, avoiding time-consuming removal and reinstallation
  • Removes carbonised deposits, oil residue, and dust from components
  • Suitable subject to isolation, method statement, and site risk assessment

IS DRY ICE BLASTING RIGHT FOR YOU?

Dry ice blasting is highly effective in the right applications, but it's not the answer to every cleaning challenge. An honest guide to help you assess suitability.

LIKELY A GOOD FIT

  • Equipment that cannot be easily dismantled for cleaning
  • Applications where water or chemical use is a problem
  • Sensitive surfaces where abrasive methods risk damage
  • Electrical equipment where moisture cannot be introduced (subject to isolation and assessment)
  • Food production requiring no added chemicals (subject to site controls)
  • Fire and smoke remediation where conventional cleaning is insufficient
  • Situations where minimising secondary waste is a priority
  • Work needed outside production hours

MAY NEED FURTHER ASSESSMENT

  • Heavy grease or thick coatings may need pre-treatment before dry ice blasting is fully effective
  • Very thick or mechanically bonded coatings may require additional or alternative methods
  • Outdoor blasting in high wind conditions — assessed case by case
  • Soft, loose, or water-soluble coatings — testing recommended
  • Surface preparation to a specific profile standard may need a different approach
  • Very large open areas may be more cost-effective with alternative methods depending on access

Common Questions

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Answers to the questions we hear most often about dry ice blasting and CO₂ cleaning.

Dry ice blasting can be used on electrical equipment when the equipment has been properly isolated and assessed for suitability. CO₂ is non-conductive and the process introduces no moisture, making it suitable for applications where water-based cleaning is not viable. All work on or near electrical equipment is subject to a risk assessment and method statement.

The CO₂ pellets sublimate on impact — converting directly from solid to gas — so no blast media remains on the surface. The only material to manage after cleaning is the original contamination that has been dislodged, typically collected and disposed of in the normal way. This often reduces secondary waste compared to methods that add sand, grit, or water.

In some applications, dry ice blasting can be carried out on equipment that is running or in standby — one of the key operational advantages. This is assessed on a case-by-case basis depending on the asset type, contamination level, access, and risk assessment. Many clients find that cleaning during a short planned stop is still significantly faster than a full strip-clean cycle.

Dry ice blasting is used in food production environments and is recognised as suitable in many such settings, subject to site-specific controls and a method statement agreed in advance. No added chemicals are used and no water is introduced. Suitability is confirmed on a per-site and per-application basis before work commences.

Downtime requirements vary considerably depending on the asset, contamination extent, and whether in-place cleaning is possible. Many clients find dry ice blasting reduces total cleaning downtime compared to strip-and-clean approaches, particularly because there is no drying time. We provide a realistic estimate after an initial assessment of your requirements.

Very thick mechanically bonded coatings, some soft or loose surface finishes, and applications requiring a specific surface profile may need alternative or combined methods. Heavy grease deposits sometimes benefit from pre-treatment. We give an honest assessment before recommending dry ice blasting — if it is not the right solution for your situation, we will tell you.

Yes — we regularly carry out cleaning work outside normal business hours and at weekends to minimise disruption to operations. Availability is subject to scheduling; please discuss your requirements and any time constraints when you get in touch.
